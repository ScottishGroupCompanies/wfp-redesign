What we install is ballistic-resistant security film: a thick multi-layer polyester laminate bonded to the interior surface of existing glass, usually paired with a mechanical attachment system. ## What Bullet Proof Window Film Actually Does to Glass

Standard annealed or tempered glass fails catastrophically. Annealed glass cracks and sheds large jagged shards; tempered glass survives harder hits but then disintegrates into fragments. Either failure mode gives an intruder an open portal in seconds.

Ballistic-resistant film changes that behavior. The film — typically 14 to 22 mil of layered polyester — holds the glass together after it cracks. Instead of a hole, you get a spiderwebbed but largely intact pane that absorbs repeated impacts and dramatically increases the effort required to defeat the opening. Security professionals call this time-to-entry, measured in minutes rather than seconds — exactly what a lockdown or police response needs.

We are always direct about the technology's boundary: film on standard glass is not rated to stop rifle fire, and no manufacturer certifies film-only glass as a ballistic barrier. What it does credibly is resist handgun fire for multiple rounds in tested configurations, defeat smash-and-grab attempts almost entirely, and hold fragments in place during blasts. When a project genuinely requires UL 752 glazing — guard booths, teller lines, high-threat entrances — we say so and spec true ballistic laminates instead.

## How Ballistic Ratings and Testing Standards Actually Work

The glazing industry does not use the word "bulletproof" in any test standard, because resistance is graded, not absolute.

- **UL 752.** The dominant North American rating for bullet-resisting barriers, Levels 1 through 10, from 9mm handgun up through armor-piercing rifle rounds. Film assemblies sit at the lower levels; Level 3+ generally requires laminated ballistic glazing.
- **NIJ ballistic-resistance levels.** The [NIJ Standard 0123.00 threat levels](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/307347.pdf) published by the National Institute of Justice give buyers a common reference for graded ballistic performance, and reputable manufacturers publish third-party test data against such protocols rather than marketing claims.
- **ANSI Z97.1 and CPSC 16 CFR 1201.** These govern safety glazing and how glass behaves when broken — relevant because film changes the fragment-retention behavior of the whole assembly.
- **GSA/ISC blast criteria.** For federal and high-value buildings in Center City and around the Navy Yard, fragment retention under blast loading is often the design driver, and film excels at it.

When we quote a ballistic project, we bring the manufacturer's test reports — which product, what ammunition, how many shots, and what happened to the witness panel behind it. If a contractor cannot produce that documentation, that is your answer about the contractor.

## The Product Landscape: Films and Attachment Systems We Spec

Not every thick film is a ballistic candidate, and the film is only half the system:

- **3M Security Series (Ultra S600, S800).** Hundreds of micro-layers of polyester give roughly double the tensile strength of standard security film at equal thickness. S800 is our flagship for ballistic and blast work where glass clarity matters.
- **LLumar Safety and Security films.** A workhorse line for commercial retrofits, with strong puncture-tear performance and broad glass-size tables for matching thickness to panes.
- **Vista and 3M combined solar-security films.** For west-facing Center City retail that needs heat rejection and security in one layer, dual-function films avoid stacking two products on one pane.
- **Mechanical attachment systems.** The detail most quotes leave out. Adhesive-only film eventually lets a cracked pane tear free at the edge; anchoring the film to the frame with a metal or engineered polymer batten locks the assembly to the structure. On every ballistic job, attachment hardware is standard, not an upsell.

Glass condition matters too. Filming cracked or poorly seated glass is wasted money, so every project starts with a pane-by-pane survey of substrate, framing integrity, and edge bite.

## The Installation Process: What We Do and What We Never Do

Proper installation is slow, clean work. We survey each pane, strip old adhesive, and polish the glass until zero residue remains — trapped grit telegraphs through thick security film immediately.

What we never do is apply film to wet glass or skip edge sealing — trapped moisture creates haze within a week, and an unsealed edge is the first place adhesion fails under impact load. We never film over failed film either; we remove and start clean. On occupied buildings we phase the work after hours so the business never closes.

Cure time is the one thing clients must respect: thick security film takes up to 30 days to fully bond and can look hazy during that window. We tell every client this in writing, because month-one appearance is not the finished product.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can window film actually stop a bullet?

Thick ballistic-resistant film on appropriate glass can resist limited handgun fire in tested configurations, holding the pane together through multiple rounds before penetration. It is not rated to stop rifle fire, and no film-only application meets bullet-resisting glazing standards. The realistic value is delayed entry and fragment retention — minutes of resistance rather than impenetrability.

### How thick does security film need to be for ballistic applications?

Serious ballistic work starts around 14 mil and typically runs to 22 mil of multi-layer polyester, paired with mechanical edge attachment. Thinner 4 to 8 mil films are excellent for smash-and-grab and fragment control but are not ballistic candidates. Thickness is always matched to glass type and pane size per the manufacturer's tables.

### Will the film make my storefront look tinted or frosted?

No. Security films are optically clear, and on clear glass most people cannot tell the film is present. Combined security-solar products add a subtle neutral tone if you want heat rejection too. We show samples against your actual glass before you commit.

### Does film work on old historic windows in Old City or Society Hill?

Often yes, but the glass survey comes first. Wavy, thin, or previously repaired historic glass has different bonding characteristics, and some deteriorated panes are better reglazed before filming. On sound historic glass, film adds shatter resistance and UV protection that helps preserve interior finishes.

### Can film be combined with alarms or cameras?

It should be. Film buys time and alarms consume it — a flexing pane that triggers a glass-break sensor turns a quiet breach into a responded event. Most of our commercial installations are specified alongside the client's alarm vendor.
