## How Security Film Actually Performs

Security films are thick, micro-layered polyester sheets — commonly 8 mil to 14 mil — bonded to the interior surface of existing glass with a high-tensile attachment system. When the glass is struck, the film holds the broken pieces together instead of letting them scatter or fall out of the frame.

Performance is measured against recognized standards, not marketing language:

- **UL 752 ratings** classify resistance to ballistic impacts at defined levels; film plus glass assemblies are tested for the number of rounds and the spall control they achieve.
- **Impact and intrusion tests** (such as ASTM-type impact protocols) measure how many strikes a filmed pane can absorb before breach — quality 8 mil security film can extend attack time from seconds to several minutes.
- **Spall control** means shards stay on the film rather than spraying occupants, which matters enormously in lobbies, classrooms, and waiting rooms.

This is the distinction worth understanding: film will not turn a storefront pane into an armored wall, but it converts a one-hit failure into a sustained barrier. ### Ballistic-Resistant vs. Bulletproof: The Honest Framing

Products are sometimes marketed carelessly, so property owners deserve a clear-eyed explanation. A filmed pane is a **ballistic-resistant assembly**, not an armor panel. Under sustained firearm fire the glass will eventually be penetrated — but it resists initial rounds, absorbs energy, controls spall, and buys critical seconds for occupants to move, shelter, or for responders to arrive. For facilities planning against smash-and-grab theft, vandalism, active-threat scenarios, or accidental impacts, that layered resistance is a meaningful and code-respectable upgrade.

Facilities teams evaluating [ballistic resistance options](/benefits/ballistic-resistance/) often combine film with laminated glass or polycarbonate for higher-threat locations, while film alone is a smart, budget-conscious layer for thousands of otherwise ordinary windows. The goal is never perfection against every threat — it is meaningful resistance sized to the realistic risks your specific property faces, installed by people who test and verify rather than guess.
