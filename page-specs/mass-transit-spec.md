### 8. FILM TYPES SLIDER (6 slides, 2752×1536)

**Slide 1 — Solar Control Film** (`mb-slider-solar.jpg`)
- Q1: Will solar film darken our terminal windows or change the open feel passengers expect?
  A1: High-VLT solar control films like 3M Prestige 70 transmit 70% of visible light — the terminal stays bright and open. The film filters infrared heat (up to 79% blocked) and UV (99% blocked) without affecting the architectural character of the space. From outside, the glass has a subtle, nearly invisible tint.
- Q2: Our gate windows face west and the afternoon sun makes the seating area unusable. What film works here?
  A2: Spectrally selective solar control film is specifically designed for this scenario. It targets the infrared portion of the solar spectrum — blocking heat — without significantly reducing visible light. Passengers near west-facing gate windows get comfortable seating; the view and bright atmosphere remain intact.
- Q3: Can film handle the large format glazing panels typical of modern airport terminals?
  A3: Yes — film is applied in panels and seamed to fit any glazing configuration, including floor-to-ceiling curtain wall panels, oversized fixed glazing, and angled glass. Our installers are experienced with the large-format commercial glazing common in PHL's terminal expansions.

**Slide 2 — Anti-Graffiti Film** (`mb-slider-antigraffiti.jpg`)
- Q1: We have underground SEPTA stations where tagging happens multiple times a week. Is film practical at that frequency?
  A1: Yes — high-frequency vandalism is exactly where sacrificial anti-graffiti film delivers the most value. The film is peeled and replaced rather than requiring glass restoration or etching removal. Replacement is fast, relatively low-cost, and can be performed during off-peak hours without closing the station.
- Q2: Does anti-graffiti film work on the stainless steel and metal surfaces in our stations, not just glass?
  A2: Yes. Sacrificial anti-graffiti film bonds to smooth metal surfaces — stainless steel elevator panels, fare gate housings, column cladding, platform mirrors — as well as glass. The same peel-and-replace approach applies regardless of the surface material.
- Q3: How does anti-graffiti film hold up to the cleaning chemicals transit agencies use?
  A3: Anti-graffiti film is resistant to the cleaning agents commonly used in transit maintenance, including industrial degreasers and pressure washing. The film is designed for high-maintenance environments. We can provide chemical compatibility documentation for your facilities team before installation.
- Internal link in Q2: `anti-graffiti window film` already in benefit row — omit from slide to avoid duplicate

**Slide 3 — Safety & Security Film** (`mb-slider-security.jpg`)
- Q1: What security film standard applies to airport and transit facilities?
  A1: 3M Scotchshield Ultra safety films are rated to ANSI Z97.1 (safety glazing) and ASTM F1642 (blast resistance). For airport and transit applications, these are the key standards. The film holds glass fragments in place on breakage — whether from an accident, structural stress, or a security incident — protecting passengers from flying shards.
- Q2: Does security film affect the appearance of our ticketing windows and security checkpoints?
  A2: Clear safety films are optically clear — staff and passengers see no difference after installation. For ticketing windows where some visual distinction is appropriate, we can combine safety film with a subtle tint or frosted lower section. The structural protection is invisible.
- Q3: Can safety film be layered with solar control film for gates that need both?
  A3: Yes. Dual-function films combine safety performance (ASTM F1642 rated) with solar control — single application that handles both heat rejection and fragment retention. This is a common specification for PHL gate windows facing runways, where both solar load and safety requirements apply.
- Internal link: `safety and security film` → `/benefits/safety-and-security/`

**Slide 4 — Decorative & Privacy Film** (`mb-slider-privacy.jpg`)
- Q1: We need visual privacy for our operations center windows that overlook the terminal. What are our options?
  A1: Frosted [decorative and privacy film](/benefits/decorative-promotional/) provides one-way visual privacy during daylight hours — staff inside see out; passengers in the terminal see an opaque surface. 3M Fasara films are available in dozens of patterns from subtle linen to bold geometric, and can be matched to your facility's design language.
- Q2: Can we use film to create wayfinding graphics or branded glass in the terminal?
  A2: Yes. Printed decorative film can carry any graphic, pattern, or brand element and is applied directly to glass. It's used in airports for gate identification graphics, sponsor walls, retail partition branding, and terminal artwork installations. Film removes cleanly when graphics need updating.
- Q3: Our security operations windows need privacy but our officers need to see the terminal floor clearly. Is there a one-way solution?
  A3: One-way mirror film achieves this during daytime: the reflective exterior side prevents passengers from seeing into the operations room, while officers inside have a clear view of the terminal. Effectiveness depends on maintaining lower light levels inside than outside — standard for most security operations centers.

**Slide 5 — Low-E Climate Control Film** (`mb-slider-lowe.jpg`)
- Q1: Our older SEPTA stations have single-pane glazing throughout. Can film improve winter performance?
  A1: Yes — Low-E insulating film (like 3M Thinsulate CC75) improves thermal performance of single-pane glass in both directions: it reflects interior heat back into the waiting area in winter and blocks solar heat gain in summer. For older Philadelphia station buildings with original single-pane glazing, Low-E film is often the most cost-effective upgrade available.
- Q2: Does Low-E film affect radio frequency transmission? We have communications equipment near station glass.
  A2: Standard Low-E films contain a thin metallic coating that can attenuate some RF signals. If this is a concern for your communications infrastructure, we specify non-metallic spectrally selective films — like 3M Prestige series — that achieve similar solar performance without affecting RF transmission. This is a common consideration for transit control facilities.
- Q3: Can Low-E and solar control film be applied to the same glass?
  A3: 3M Thinsulate CC75 is a dual-function film that delivers both solar control in summer and improved insulation in winter in a single application. It's designed for year-round climates like Philadelphia's — hot summers and cold winters — making it one installation that addresses both seasonal challenges.

**Slide 6 — Exterior Window Film** (`mb-slider-daylight.jpg`)
- Q1: Our station canopy glass is exposed to weather and cleaning equipment. Can film be applied externally?
  A1: Yes. [Exterior-mounted window film](/benefits/exterior-window-film/) is specifically engineered for outdoor exposure — UV-stabilized coating, scratch-resistant surface, and durability against weather cycling. It's used on station canopy glazing, skylight panels, and external curtain wall sections where interior installation isn't accessible.
- Q2: How long does exterior film last compared to interior film?
  A2: Quality exterior film has a manufacturer-warranted lifespan of 7–10 years in outdoor exposure, compared to 10–15 years for interior film. In transit environments with regular pressure washing, we specify the appropriate film grade for the cleaning protocol used by your maintenance team.
- Q3: Can exterior film be applied while the station remains operational?
  A3: Yes. Exterior application accesses glass from outside the station — no disruption to passengers or operations inside. Installation is scheduled around maintenance windows, track access hours, or off-peak periods to minimize any impact on transit operations.

### 9. CASE STUDY
- **Location:** 30th Street Station, 2955 Market Street, West Philadelphia
- **Property:** Amtrak's principal Philadelphia terminal — the grand Beaux-Arts station serves Amtrak, SEPTA Regional Rail, and Greyhound, with approximately 4 million annual passengers
- **Challenge:** The station's south-facing Palladian windows and upper clerestory glazing generated extreme solar heat gain in the main waiting hall during summer months. Seating near the south-facing windows became too hot to use during peak afternoon hours. Simultaneously, exposed exterior glazing on the east-facing concourse level was subject to repeated graffiti incidents requiring costly restoration.
- **Solution:** 3M Prestige 70 solar control film applied to south-facing clerestory and upper-hall glazing — high VLT preserving the hall's historic character while blocking 79% of solar heat. Sacrificial anti-graffiti film applied to exterior concourse-level glass panels on the east elevation.
- **Result:** Main hall seating near south windows returned to full use year-round. Zero graffiti restoration costs on treated exterior panels since installation. Facilities team reported reduced HVAC cycling during summer peak hours.
