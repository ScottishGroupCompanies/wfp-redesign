## Unique Angle
Philadelphia's Benjamin Franklin Parkway hosts one of the highest concentrations of world-class museums anywhere — Barnes Foundation, Philadelphia Museum of Art, Franklin Institute, Rodin Museum, Penn Museum. The Free Library of Philadelphia operates 54 branches. The primary driver is artifact/collection preservation: UV degradation of pigments, textiles, and paper is irreversible. The American Institute for Conservation sets the standard: UV exposure below 75 lux and UV radiation below 10 μW/lm. Window film is the most cost-effective path to AIC compliance.

## Benefit Rows (4)
1. **UV & Artifact Preservation** — 99% UV rejection meets AIC conservation standards; prevents irreversible fading of pigments, textiles, paper, and organic materials
2. **Glare Control for Visitors** — eliminates wash-out on artwork and display screens; improves reading comfort in library stacks without losing natural light
3. **Energy Management for Collections Climate** — reduces solar heat gain that stresses HVAC systems maintaining collection-safe humidity and temperature ranges
4. **Security Film** — anti-smash-and-grab for display cases; forced entry delay at public-facing entries; protects both collections and visitors

## Case Study
Location: University City, Philadelphia — university research library
Challenge: South-facing reading room with intense afternoon glare preventing use of study areas; faculty complaints about UV risk to rare book collection housed near windows; energy costs elevated by uncontrolled solar heat gain
Solution: 3M Prestige 70 solar control on south-facing reading room glass; LLumar UV conservation film on rare book room windows; glare reduction maintained natural light character
Result: Reading room reclaimed as usable study space all afternoon; UV exposure at rare book room windows reduced to AIC-compliant levels; measurable cooling load reduction
