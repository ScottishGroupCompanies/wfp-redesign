### 8. FILM TYPES SLIDER (6 slides, `he-slider-*.jpg` 2752×1536)

**Slide 1 — UV Protection Film**
- Q: How does window film protect patients in sun-facing hospital rooms? A: UV-blocking films stop up to 99% of ultraviolet radiation before it enters the patient room. This reduces UV exposure for patients in recovery, protects wound-healing skin from unnecessary light stress, and slows fading of medical-grade upholstery and curtains — all without blocking the natural light that supports patient well-being.
- Q: Will UV film change how our patient rooms look? A: High-VLT UV films are virtually invisible — rooms stay bright and welcoming. Patients and staff won't notice the film, but the UV protection is immediate and measurable from day one.
- Q: Does UV film affect plants or therapy lighting in patient rooms? A: UV film blocks harmful UV wavelengths while allowing the visible light spectrum to pass through. Most therapeutic uses of natural light — including plant care and mood support — are unaffected.

**Slide 2 — Energy Efficiency Film**
- Q: Which hospital areas benefit most from solar control film? A: South and west-facing patient wings, glass-walled atria, large lobby windows, and cafeteria glazing see the greatest impact — these areas generate the most solar heat gain and place the heaviest load on HVAC systems.
- Q: Can solar control film help our hospital meet sustainability targets? A: Yes. Solar control film contributes to ENERGY STAR performance improvements and supports sustainability reporting by reducing cooling energy consumption across large glass footprints. We provide documentation for energy audits and green building certifications.
- Q: Is window film installation disruptive to hospital operations? A: No. Window film is applied from inside with no fumes, no structural work, and no noise beyond a normal maintenance visit. Individual rooms or corridors can be scheduled around patient care, shift changes, or facility operations.

**Slide 3 — Privacy Film**
- Q: What areas of a hospital typically use frosted privacy film? A: Consultation rooms, exam rooms, nurse stations, pharmacy windows, behavioral health unit corridors, administrative offices, and patient intake areas — any glazed space where visual privacy supports clinical professionalism and patient dignity.
- Q: Is frosted film HIPAA-compliant? A: Window film addresses visual privacy, which is one component of creating a HIPAA-aware environment. We recommend evaluating your specific facility layout with your compliance team — film is a cost-effective tool for resolving glass-based visibility concerns.
- Q: Can frosted film be branded or patterned for our facility? A: Yes. Custom-patterned and branded films are available for lobbies, reception areas, and wayfinding applications — combining privacy with professional healthcare branding.

**Slide 4 — Safety & Security Film**
- Q: What film grade is appropriate for pharmacy windows? A: For pharmacy and controlled-substance windows, we typically recommend 8–14 mil safety film meeting ANSI Z97.1 and CPSC 16 CFR Part 1201. We assess your specific window construction and threat profile before specifying a film grade.
- Q: Can security film help in behavioral health units? A: Yes. Safety film holds glass fragments in place after impact, significantly reducing the risk of glass breakage becoming a safety incident. Combined with appropriate window frame attachment systems, it adds a meaningful layer of protection to glazed behavioral health spaces.
- Q: Does safety film affect the optical clarity of pharmacy windows? A: Clear safety films are optically transparent — pharmacy staff can see through them normally. Tinted safety options are also available when both security and solar control are required simultaneously.

**Slide 5 — Solar Control & Glare Film**
- Q: Can window film reduce glare in hospital waiting areas? A: Yes. Waiting areas with large south or west-facing windows benefit significantly from solar control film — reducing the harsh glare that makes extended waiting uncomfortable without blocking the natural light that supports a calm, welcoming environment.
- Q: Will solar control film affect clinical lighting in treatment areas? A: We select VLT levels based on the specific room's orientation and clinical requirements. Treatment rooms requiring consistent artificial light are typically filmed conservatively; patient rooms and waiting areas can accommodate higher VLT reductions.
- Q: Does film installation interfere with medical equipment near windows? A: No. Film is applied to glass surfaces only and does not generate EMI, RF interference, or any emissions that affect medical equipment. Installation is a surface-only process.

**Slide 6 — Decorative & Architectural Film**
- Q: Can window film reinforce our facility's design and wayfinding? A: Yes. Custom-patterned and branded films work with your existing design language — reinforcing wayfinding, separating clinical zones visually, and creating a professional, cohesive appearance across large glazed areas.
- Q: Is decorative film appropriate for healthcare lobbies and atria? A: Absolutely. Healthcare lobbies and atria often benefit from patterned or gradient frosted films that soften large glass surfaces, reduce glare in open areas, and create a calmer, more patient-centered environment.
- Q: Can decorative film be removed if we renovate or rebrand? A: Yes. Decorative films are removable without damaging underlying glass, making them a significantly less expensive and disruptive alternative to acid-etched or sandblasted glass.
