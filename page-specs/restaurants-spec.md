### 8. FILM TYPES SLIDER (6 slides, 2752×1536 images)
3 real Q&A items per slide — restaurant-specific.

**Slide 1 — Solar Control Film** (`rt-slider-solar.jpg`)
- Film name: Solar Control / 3M Prestige Series
- Q1: Will solar film make my dining room feel dark or cave-like?
  A1: No — high-VLT films like 3M Prestige 70 transmit 70% of visible light while blocking up to 79% of solar heat. Your dining room keeps its bright, airy feel; guests near windows won't squint.
- Q2: Our west-facing windows make the 5–8pm dinner rush unbearable. Can film fix that?
  A2: That's the most common restaurant request we get in Philadelphia. Solar control film reduces glare by 70%+ — enough to eliminate guest complaints and keep west-facing booths fully occupied through sunset service.
- Q3: Will the film affect how food looks? Colors matter in a dining room.
  A3: High-VLT films have a Color Rendering Index (CRI) above 90 for most 3M Prestige series — nearly identical to unfiltered glass. Plate presentation, table settings, and ambient lighting all look natural.

**Slide 2 — Decorative & Privacy Film** (`rt-slider-privacy.jpg`)
- Film name: Decorative / 3M Fasara Series
- Q1: We want frosted glass partitions between dining sections. Can film do that?
  A1: Absolutely. 3M Fasara decorative films come in hundreds of patterns and apply directly to existing glass. You can create private dining areas without structural changes or new glass walls.
- Q2: Can we put our logo or a custom pattern on the glass?
  A2: Yes. Printed decorative film can be cut to any shape — popular for front windows, host stand glass, and wine room dividers. It removes cleanly without damaging glass when you rebrand.
- Q3: We want some privacy at street level without blocking natural light above.
  A3: Dual-zone application: frosted on the lower 4 feet (pedestrian eye level) with clear or solar control film above. You get privacy where it matters while keeping the upper portion bright and open.

**Slide 3 — Safety & Security Film** (`rt-slider-security.jpg`)
- Film name: Safety & Security / 3M Scotchshield Ultra Series
- Q1: We've had incidents with glass breakage in the dining room. Is security film obvious?
  A1: Clear safety films are completely invisible — guests can't tell it's there. 3M Scotchshield Ultra S800 holds glass shards in place if breakage occurs from accidents, severe weather, or forced entry.
- Q2: Does safety film help with smash-and-grab at the bar?
  A2: Yes. Safety film dramatically slows forced entry by holding the glass intact even after it cracks. It's especially relevant for street-level bars, wine displays, and POS areas near the entrance.
- Q3: Do we need to treat all windows or just the vulnerable ones?
  A3: Most restaurants prioritize street-facing windows and doors, wine room glass, and POS stations. We assess your space and recommend targeted coverage — not every pane needs the same film.
- Internal link: `safety and security film` → `/benefits/safety-and-security/`

**Slide 4 — Anti-Graffiti Film** (`rt-slider-antigraffiti.jpg`)
- Film name: Anti-Graffiti / Sacrificial Film
- Q1: We're on a high foot-traffic street and we've had tagging issues. What's the solution?
  A1: Sacrificial anti-graffiti film acts as a removable protective layer. If someone tags your glass, we peel and replace the film — no acid-washing or costly glass replacement. Your windows look new every time.
- Q2: Does anti-graffiti film affect clarity from inside the dining room?
  A2: The optical clarity is nearly identical to bare glass. Guests dining near the windows can't tell the difference, and the exterior appearance is unchanged.
- Q3: How often does anti-graffiti film need to be replaced?
  A3: In clean environments, 5–7 years. On high-traffic corridors like South Street or Frankford Ave, replacement depends on incidents — but each swap takes about 15 minutes and costs a fraction of glass restoration.
- Internal link: `anti-graffiti window film` → `/benefits/anti-graffiti/`

**Slide 5 — Low-E Climate Control Film** (`rt-slider-lowe.jpg`)
- Film name: Low-E / Thinsulate Series
- Q1: Our building has old single-pane windows. Can film help with winter heating costs too?
  A1: Yes — Low-E window film works year-round. It reflects heat back into the dining room in winter and blocks solar heat gain in summer. Philadelphia's cold winters and hot summers make Low-E films especially effective for restaurant buildings with original single-pane glass.
- Q2: Our HVAC runs constantly near the window walls. Will film reduce that load?
  A2: Significantly. Solar control films cut solar heat gain through glass by up to 79%, directly reducing cooling load. For open kitchen layouts where heat and glass exposure combine, the impact on comfort is noticeable.
- Q3: What's the difference between Low-E film and standard solar control film?
  A3: Standard solar control film addresses summer heat and UV. Low-E film (like 3M Thinsulate CC75) also improves thermal performance in winter by reflecting interior heat back into the space — higher ROI for restaurants with year-round glass exposure.

**Slide 6 — Daylight Redirecting Film** (`rt-slider-daylight.jpg`)
- Film name: Daylight Redirecting / 3M DRF
- Q1: Our dining room gets harsh direct sun in the morning. Can film soften it?
  A1: That's exactly what 3M Daylight Redirecting Film is designed for. It redirects direct sunlight up toward the ceiling, distributing it as soft diffused light — no harsh shafts, no dark spots, and no artificial lighting needed at midday.
- Q2: Does daylight film work for cafes that want a bright, airy feel?
  A2: Yes. DRF actually increases perceived brightness by distributing light more evenly. The room feels lighter than with clear glass at peak hours — popular in breakfast/brunch spots and cafes that depend on a welcoming morning atmosphere.
- Q3: What does it look like from outside?
  A3: DRF has a subtle, slightly matte appearance from the exterior — similar to a light frost. It doesn't look like a mirror or dark tint. On brick streetscapes in Philadelphia's historic neighborhoods, it's largely indistinguishable from the interior.

### 9. CASE STUDY (of-project CSS)

Guests near the front windows were squinting and requesting table changes; the host team began pre-emptively seating parties away from the windows. - **Result:** Guest complaints about glare eliminated in the first week of service after installation. West-facing booths returned to full occupancy across the 5–8pm window. Upholstery showed no color shift at 12-month inspection. The host team stopped pre-routing guests away from the front of the restaurant.
