### 8. FILM TYPES SLIDER (6 slides, `sc-slider-*.jpg` 2752×1536)
Each slide: 3 real Q&A pairs specific to schools.

**Slide 1 — Safety & Security Film**
- Q: Will security film prevent someone from breaking through a window? A: Security film significantly increases the time and effort required to breach glass — it holds shattered glass in place rather than creating a clear opening. While no film is a guaranteed barrier, it substantially slows forced entry and buys critical response time for students and staff.
- Q: Does security film change the appearance of our school windows? A: No. Clear safety films are optically transparent — from inside or outside the building, treated glass looks identical to untreated glass. Tinted security options are also available if solar control is a simultaneous goal.
- Q: What glass thickness and film spec is appropriate for a school? A: For school entry points and ground-floor classrooms, we typically recommend an 8–14 mil safety film meeting ANSI Z97.1. We assess your specific glass type and threat profile before recommending a film grade.

**Slide 2 — UV Protection Film**
- Q: How much UV does window film actually block in a classroom? A: Our UV-blocking films block up to 99% of ultraviolet radiation across both UVA and UVB spectra — the same UV that causes fading in furnishings and contributes to cumulative eye and skin exposure for students at window-side desks.
- Q: Will UV film darken our classrooms? A: Not necessarily. UV rejection is largely independent of visible light transmission. Many of our films block 99% UV while maintaining 70% or more of natural daylight — classrooms stay bright while UV exposure drops to near zero.
- Q: Does UV film help with LEED or sustainability certification? A: Yes. UV and solar control films contribute to LEED points under energy efficiency and occupant comfort categories. We can provide documentation for your district's sustainability reporting.

**Slide 3 — Solar Control & Glare Film**
- Q: Can we get different levels of tint for different classrooms? A: Absolutely. We select VLT (visible light transmission) levels based on each room's orientation, use, and existing light levels. A south-facing computer lab may need a darker film than a north-facing art room.
- Q: Will solar control film help with our smartboard glare problem? A: Yes — this is one of the most common reasons schools call us. Reducing VLT by 20–30% on west-facing classroom windows eliminates afternoon smartboard washout without requiring blinds that block the view entirely.
- Q: How long does glare reduction film last? A: Professionally installed solar control film typically carries a 10–15 year manufacturer warranty. With proper cleaning, most films remain effective for 15–20+ years in educational settings.

**Slide 4 — Energy Efficiency (Low-E) Film**
- Q: How much can window film reduce our school's energy bills? A: Results vary by building envelope, HVAC efficiency, and window area — but solar control film can reduce cooling loads by up to 79%, which translates to meaningful utility savings in large school buildings with significant glass exposure.
- Q: Does film work in winter as well, or only for summer cooling? A: Low-emissivity (Low-E) films reduce heat loss in winter as well as heat gain in summer — improving year-round thermal performance. Philadelphia schools benefit from both cooling savings in summer and heating retention in winter.
- Q: Can we apply film during the school year? A: Yes. Professional film installation requires no structural work and minimal disruption — individual rooms can typically be done in a few hours, outside of school hours if preferred.

**Slide 5 — Decorative & Privacy Film**
- Q: Can we use frosted film on administrative office and counselor windows for privacy? A: Yes — frosted and patterned Fasara-style films are a common application in schools for counselor offices, nurse's stations, and administrative areas that require visual privacy without sacrificing natural light.
- Q: Can we add school branding or logos to window film? A: Branded and custom-printed decorative films are available for lobby windows, gymnasium facades, and main entrance glass — reinforcing school identity while serving a functional purpose.
- Q: Is decorative film removable if we redecorate or rebrand? A: Yes. Decorative films can be removed and replaced without damaging the underlying glass, making them a cost-effective alternative to permanent treatments or frosted glass replacement.

**Slide 6 — Anti-Graffiti Film**
- Q: What surfaces does anti-graffiti film protect? A: Anti-graffiti film protects glass, metal, and mirrors — not just windows. In school settings, this includes bathroom mirrors, stainless steel surfaces in restrooms, and glass entry vestibules — any smooth surface vulnerable to etching or marker vandalism.
- Q: How does anti-graffiti film work? A: The film acts as a sacrificial layer. If graffiti is applied to the film surface, the film is simply removed and replaced — at a fraction of the cost of replacing etched glass or refinishing a scratched stainless steel panel.
- Q: How often does the film need to be replaced? A: In typical school environments, anti-graffiti film in high-traffic areas (bathroom mirrors, vestibule glass) may be replaced annually or as needed. The cost per replacement is significantly lower than glass or metal surface restoration.
